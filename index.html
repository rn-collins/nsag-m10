Why This Module Exists

Healthcare built-environment research is unusually well-developed — several hundred studies now link specific, measurable design parameters to patient and staff outcomes: overnight noise levels to sleep disruption and delirium risk, lighting schedules to circadian regulation and mood, single-room versus shared accommodation to infection rates and privacy-related distress, wayfinding complexity to stress and missed appointments in cognitively vulnerable patients, and nature access to recovery time and stress markers.

Despite the evidence base, most capital-planning decisions in healthcare facilities are still made primarily on cost, code compliance, and throughput — not on the acoustic, lighting, privacy, wayfinding, nature, and sensory standards this module measures. That gap is a governance gap, not an evidence gap: the research exists; the requirement to act on it in the next design brief usually does not. An institution that governs all six dimensions has moved evidence-based design from something an individual clinician advocates for to a standing requirement every capital project has to satisfy.
Last reviewed August 2026. Evidence-based design standards and accreditation requirements (for example, Magnet and facility guidelines) continue to be updated — confirm current standards with your accreditation body before finalizing capital design briefs.
